## What the guidelines say about length

The *Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans* care about the weekly total, not the length of each session:

- Bouts of activity of any length count toward the total. The 2008 rule that activity had to last at least 10 minutes was removed.
- The total amount of activity matters more than how long each session lasts, and it can be spread across the day or week.
- No specific amount of time is recommended for muscle strengthening. One set of 8 to 12 reps per exercise is effective; 2 or 3 sets may be more effective.

## Fitting in 150 minutes

| Sessions a week | Moderate cardio per session |
| --- | --- |
| 3 | 50 minutes |
| 5 | 30 minutes |
| 7 | About 21 minutes |

For vigorous activity, halve these: 75 minutes a week is three 25-minute sessions.

## How long a strength session takes

Session length mostly comes from how many sets you do and how long you rest. Here's the arithmetic for a common full-body session, using ACSM's suggested 1 to 2 minutes of rest for muscle growth:

| Session | Sets | Time per set (work + rest) | Lifting time | With a 5 to 10-minute warm-up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 exercises × 1 set | 8 | About 100 s (40 s + 60 s rest) | About 13 min | About 20 min |
| 6 exercises × 2 sets | 12 | About 130 s (40 s + 90 s rest) | About 26 min | About 35 min |
| 6 exercises × 3 sets | 18 | About 130 s | About 39 min | About 45–50 min |

These are estimates; supersets (pairing exercises for different muscles and resting less) cut the time.

## Short or long: which is better?

Neither on its own. For muscle, what matters most is sets per muscle per week, about 10 according to ACSM's 2026 update. You can do that in three 45-minute sessions or five 25-minute ones. See [how many sets and reps to build muscle](/blog/how-many-sets-and-reps-to-build-muscle).

Choose the length you can repeat every week. A plan you skip doesn't count.
