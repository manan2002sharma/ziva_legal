## All-in-one apps or two specialists?

Most workout apps plan training and stop there, and most meal planners plan food and stop there. Of the workout apps we looked at (Fitbod, Planfit, Jefit, Ladder, Future and Freeletics), none builds a grocery list or plans meals in the same app. Freeletics comes closest, selling nutrition as a bundle with a separate app.

A handful of apps do both. They split into two groups: fitness programs that include meal plans (Centr, Sweat, BetterMe, Noom), and planners that build meals and training from your own numbers (Strongr Fastr, Siloh, ZivaAI).

The cost of two apps isn't only money. Two sets of answers drift apart: your meal planner doesn't know you added a leg day, and your workout app doesn't know you're eating 500 calories under maintenance.

## Strongr Fastr: the long-running, low-cost option

Strongr Fastr builds a weekly meal plan from simple recipes to hit your calorie and macro targets, with a grocery list, and pairs it with home or gym workouts that adapt as you progress. It supports keto, paleo, vegetarian and vegan diets, common allergies, and well-known lifting programs such as 5/3/1 and PHUL. Full meal planning is in Pro, which has a free trial; its App Store listing shows an Annual Pro Plan at $49.99 and a monthly plan at $9.99, with other Pro prices up to $79.99.

**Best for:** people who want both plans for the lowest price and don't mind logging food to fine-tune them.

## Centr and Sweat: trainer-led programs with meal plans

Centr, from Chris Hemsworth's team, and Sweat, built around women's training programs, are workout apps first. Both add meal plans and recipes; Centr picks your daily workout and meals from your goals. Centr's App Store listing shows 12 months for $119.99; Sweat's shows a yearly plan at $134.99. Both offer a 7-day free trial.

**Best for:** people who want to follow a named trainer's program and are happy to pick from a recipe library rather than have meals built to exact numbers.

## BetterMe: a broad wellness program

BetterMe combines workouts, meal planning and habit tools. Its price depends on the plan you're offered after a quiz; the App Store lists in-app purchases from $6.99 to $41.99.

## Siloh: meals that follow your training split

Siloh launched on iPhone in July 2026 and is built around the same idea as ZivaAI: one plan instead of two apps. It writes a 14-day meal plan and a 4-week training program from a few questions, with higher carbs on training days and fewer on rest days. Pro adds the AI-built program and an AI coach you can chat with, including sending photos of a restaurant menu or of what you ate. The grocery list can go to Kroger or Instacart.

Food logging, recipes, run tracking and the grocery list are free; Pro costs $19.99 a month or $149.99 a year with a 7-day trial. Android is listed as coming soon.

**Best for:** people who want meals matched to training days, food logging, and a free tier to start with.

## ZivaAI: one plan from one set of answers

ZivaAI asks about your goal, diet, allergies, the cuisines you like, the days you can train, your equipment and any sore joints. From those answers it writes a week of meals with recipes and macros, workouts of 20 to 60 minutes, and one grocery list sorted by aisle.

Because it's one plan, a change in one place carries through. Ask Ziva, the in-app coach, to make Thursday vegetarian and the grocery list changes with it.

It also logs what actually happened: photograph or describe a meal for a calorie and macro estimate, tick off sets with last session's numbers beside them, and take a daily progress photo.

**The trade-offs:** it costs {{price_yearly}} a year, the most of any option here; there's no free trial; it's iPhone and iPad only; and while it logs your meals and sets, it doesn't yet change the plan from what you log.

## Noom: behavior coaching with meal plans and classes

Noom Weight is a behavior-change program with meal plans, dietitian-backed recipes, Noom Move workout classes and optional one-to-one human coaching. A 12-month plan costs $209. Noom also sells separate medical weight-loss programs.

**Best for:** people who want lessons and a human coach more than a detailed training plan.

## Freeletics: AI training with an optional nutrition bundle

Freeletics' AI Training Coach builds bodyweight, weights and running workouts from your feedback. Nutrition comes in the Training & Nutrition bundle through a separate Freeletics Nutrition app. Plans run 3, 6 or 12 months, with no monthly option.

## MacroFactor plus MacroFactor Workouts

MacroFactor adjusts your calorie and macro targets each week from what you log. Its workout app is separate, and both together cost $89.99 a year. It's the cheapest pairing, but neither app writes your meals for you.

## Two apps: Fitbod plus Eat This Much

Fitbod builds strength workouts that adapt to your recovery and progress, and Eat This Much builds meal plans with a grocery list. Together they're the strongest pair of specialists, at roughly $140 to $156 a year.

## Which should you choose?

- **Lowest-cost all-in-one:** Strongr Fastr.
- **A trainer's program with recipes:** Centr or Sweat.
- **Lowest-cost tracking pair, happy to log food:** MacroFactor plus its Workouts app.
- **The best specialist in each area:** Fitbod plus Eat This Much.
- **Coaching and behavior change:** Noom.
- **Meals and training in one plan, with a free tier and food logging:** Siloh.
- **Meals, training and groceries planned together, changeable by chat:** ZivaAI.
