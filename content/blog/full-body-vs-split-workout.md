## What the research says

Two meta-analyses by Schoenfeld and colleagues frame the answer:

- **2016:** across 10 studies, training a muscle twice a week produced more growth than once a week (effect sizes 0.49 vs 0.30). Whether three times beats twice was unclear.
- **2019:** when weekly volume was equated, there was no significant difference between higher and lower frequency. The authors concluded people can choose the frequency they prefer.

The two fit together: training more often mostly helps because it makes it easier to do more total sets. ACSM's 2026 update recommends training all major muscle groups at least twice a week.

## The common layouts

| Split | Days a week | Each muscle trained | Session length | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Full body | 2 to 3 | 2 to 3 times | Longer | Beginners, busy weeks |
| Upper/lower | 4 | 2 times | Moderate | Most intermediate lifters |
| Push/pull/legs | 3 | Once | Moderate | Fewer days, but once a week |
| Push/pull/legs, twice | 6 | 2 times | Moderate | High-volume training |
| Body-part ("bro") split | 5 | Once | Moderate | People who like one focus a day |

## How to choose

1. **Start from your days.** Two or three days: full body. Four: upper/lower. Five or six: push/pull/legs twice or a hybrid.
2. **Check weekly sets.** Aim for about 10 sets per muscle group a week, as in [how many sets and reps to build muscle](/blog/how-many-sets-and-reps-to-build-muscle).
3. **Pick what you'll keep doing.** Since results are similar at equal volume, the split you enjoy and can repeat is the best one.
