## Start with the daily total

Per-meal targets only make sense once you know your daily number. The US Dietary Guidelines for 2025 to 2030 set a goal of **1.2 to 1.6 g of protein per kg of body weight a day** and tell people to prioritize protein at every meal. For building muscle, a 2018 meta-analysis found gains from extra protein leveled off around **1.6 g per kg a day**, with some people benefiting up to about 2.2 g.

Work out your own range with our [protein calculator](/tools/protein-calculator).

## The per-meal rule

Schoenfeld and Aragon's 2018 review suggests eating about **0.4 g per kg at each meal, across at least four meals**. That adds up to 1.6 g per kg a day. To reach the upper end of 2.2 g per kg, they suggest up to about 0.55 g per kg per meal.

| Body weight | 0.4 g/kg per meal | 0.55 g/kg per meal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 120 lb (54 kg) | 22 g | 30 g |
| 140 lb (64 kg) | 25 g | 35 g |
| 160 lb (73 kg) | 29 g | 40 g |
| 180 lb (82 kg) | 33 g | 45 g |
| 200 lb (91 kg) | 36 g | 50 g |
| 220 lb (100 kg) | 40 g | 55 g |

## Is there a limit per meal?

The old idea that your body can only use about 20 to 30 g at a time doesn't hold up. In a 2023 trial, 36 healthy young men took 0, 25 or 100 g of milk protein after a full-body workout. The 100 g dose produced a larger muscle-building response that lasted more than 12 hours, with muscle protein synthesis about 20% higher in the first 4 hours and about 40% higher from 4 to 12 hours than with 25 g.

Read that carefully. It was a single session in young men, it didn't measure muscle growth over weeks, and one author works for a dairy company. It shows a big meal isn't wasted. It doesn't show that one huge meal beats four moderate ones over months.

The practical takeaway: hit your daily total first, and spreading it over three to five meals makes that easier.

## What 30 grams looks like

Protein per USDA serving, from FoodData Central:

| Food | Serving | Protein |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tempeh | 1 cup (166 g) | 34 g |
| Firm tofu | ½ cup (126 g) | 22 g |
| Lentils, cooked | 1 cup (198 g) | 18 g |
| Plain nonfat Greek yogurt | 170 g pot | 17 g |
| Eggs, hard-boiled | 3 large | 19 g |
| Milk, 2% | 1 cup | 8 g |

So three eggs plus a pot of Greek yogurt is about 36 g, and a cup of lentils with half a cup of tofu is about 40 g.

## Plant protein counts too

A 2019 review of vegetarian diets found plant and animal proteins differ in digestibility by only a few percent. It also found no need to combine plant proteins in the same meal: variety over the day is enough. For specific foods, see [high-protein vegetarian foods](/blog/high-protein-vegetarian-foods).
