## Why the grocery list matters

The plan is only half the job. The other half is shopping for it: adding up every ingredient across the week, sorting it by aisle, and not buying three bunches of coriander. A good planner does that maths for you. Mealime was loved for exactly this, and it shut down on October 21, 2026.

## Samsung Food: best free list

The free version plans your weekly meals, turns them into a shopping list and lets you share it with family. It can also hand the list to grocery delivery from a set of retailers. Food+ adds AI meal plans and nutrition tracking.

## Eat This Much and Fitia: lists from calorie-targeted plans

Both build meal plans around calorie and macro targets and produce the list from them. Eat This Much emails the weekly plan with its grocery list; Fitia builds an automatic shopping list in Premium and adds food logging.

## MyFitnessPal Premium+: list synced to grocery apps

MyFitnessPal's top tier adds a meal planner, recipes and grocery-app syncing. It's the natural pick if you already track food there.

## ZivaAI: one list for the whole planned week

ZivaAI adds up every ingredient from your planned meals into one list for the week, sorted by aisle, with how much of each you need and how many meals use it. You tick items off in the store or share the list, and a reminder fires on your shopping day. If you ask Ziva to change a meal, the list changes with it.

**The trade-offs:** no free tier or trial, and iPhone and iPad only. It's built for people who want workouts planned alongside meals; if you only want recipes and a list, Samsung Food is cheaper.

## Which should you choose?

- **Free:** Samsung Food.
- **Calorie targets:** Eat This Much or Fitia.
- **Already on MyFitnessPal:** Premium+.
- **Meals and workouts planned together, with the list kept in sync:** ZivaAI.
