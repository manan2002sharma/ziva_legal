## What Eat This Much does well

Eat This Much builds meal plans automatically from your calorie and macro goals, the foods you like, your budget and your schedule. Premium costs $14.99 a month or $59.99 a year, plans the whole week and emails it with a grocery list. Logging what you eat is free.

People look elsewhere for three reasons: the free tier only plans one day at a time, it doesn't plan workouts, and family members with different targets aren't individually optimized.

## Prospre: the closest match on macros

Prospre builds a week of meals from your goals and favorite foods to any calorie, protein, fat and carb target, and tells you what to buy. Most of its App Store yearly plans are $49.99, with one at $79.99, and monthly plans run $9.99 to $14.99.

## Fitia: macro plans plus food logging

Fitia builds plans around calorie and macro targets for weight loss or muscle gain, adds an automatic shopping list, and lets you log food by photo, voice, text or barcode in Premium. The App Store lists a month at $19.99 and a year at $59.99, the same yearly price as Eat This Much.

## Samsung Food: cheapest

Samsung Food's free version plans your week and builds a shareable shopping list. Food+ adds AI meal plans for health goals and nutrition tracking for $6.99 a month or $59.99 a year.

## Plan to Eat and Paprika: if you'd rather plan your own recipes

Some people leave Eat This Much because they want their own recipes, not generated ones. Plan to Eat imports recipes from the web, puts them on a calendar and builds the shopping list, for $54.99 a year after a 14-day free trial. Paprika does the same with an aisle-sorted grocery list and pantry tracking, for $4.99 on iPhone, paid once, with an optional Premium in-app purchase. Neither sets calorie or macro targets for you.

## MyFitnessPal Premium+: if you already log there

The meal planner, 1,500-plus recipes and grocery-app syncing are only in Premium+. If your history is already in MyFitnessPal, it saves you moving.

## ZivaAI: if you want workouts planned too

ZivaAI plans meals with recipes and macros, workouts of 20 to 60 minutes for your equipment, and one grocery list, all from the same answers. You change the plan by asking Ziva, its coach, and the grocery list follows. To log what you ate, tick a planned meal or photograph anything else for an estimate; there's no barcode scanner.
