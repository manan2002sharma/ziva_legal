## The difference in one line

Eat This Much decides what you'll eat and hands you a grocery list. MacroFactor watches what you actually eat and your weight, then adjusts your calorie and macro targets each week.

## Side by side

| | Eat This Much | MacroFactor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main job | Automatic meal plans | Food tracking with adaptive targets |
| Meal plans | Yes; weekly with Premium | No |
| Grocery list | Yes, with Premium | No |
| Food logging | Free; reads nutrition labels from photos | Barcode, label scan and AI photo |
| Adjusts your targets | You set the targets | Yes, weekly check-ins from your logs |
| Workouts | No | Separate MacroFactor Workouts app |
| Free option | One-day plans; 14-day Premium trial | 7-day trial, no free tier |
| US price | $14.99/month or $59.99/year | $11.99/month or $71.99/year; $89.99/year with Workouts |

## Choose Eat This Much if

You don't want to think about what to eat. You set calories, macros, foods you like, budget and schedule, and it writes the meals. It's the cheaper of the two per year.

## Choose MacroFactor if

You're happy to log and want your targets to keep up with you. Its weekly check-ins adjust calories from your actual intake and weight, which suits longer cuts and bulks.
