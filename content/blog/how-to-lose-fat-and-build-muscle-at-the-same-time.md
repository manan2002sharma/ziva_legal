## Yes, and not only for beginners

A 2020 review by Barakat and colleagues looked at whether trained people can recomp. It found recomposition documented in novices and people with overweight or obesity, and also in a substantial body of research on resistance-trained people. It named two drivers: progressive resistance training and evidence-based nutrition.

## What protein does

The clearest numbers come from a 2016 trial by Longland and colleagues. Forty young men ate about 40% below their energy needs for 4 weeks while doing strength training and interval training six days a week.

| Group | Protein | Lean mass | Fat mass |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Higher protein | 2.4 g per kg a day | +1.2 kg | −4.8 kg |
| Lower protein | 1.2 g per kg a day | +0.1 kg | −3.5 kg |

Both groups lost fat, but only the higher-protein group gained meaningful muscle. Keep the setup in mind: a 40% deficit with six training days a week is extreme, it lasted 4 weeks, and it was closely supervised. Don't copy the deficit; copy the lesson about protein.

## A realistic recomp plan

1. **Lift progressively.** Train every major muscle at least twice a week with about 10 sets per muscle a week, and add weight or reps over time. See [how many sets and reps to build muscle](/blog/how-many-sets-and-reps-to-build-muscle).
2. **Eat a moderate deficit, or around maintenance.** Weight-loss guidelines typically use a deficit of 500 to 750 kcal a day. For recomp, many people use less than that. See [how big a calorie deficit should be](/blog/calorie-deficit-how-much).
3. **Keep protein high.** About 1.6 to 2.2 g per kg a day, spread over meals. Use our [protein calculator](/tools/protein-calculator).
4. **Measure more than weight.** Waist, photos and lifts tell you whether it's working when the scale doesn't.

## Coming back after a break

If you used to train, you may regain lost muscle quickly. In a 2024 trial, adults who trained for 10 weeks, stopped for 10 and then trained again regained lost muscle size and strength rapidly, ending level with people who never stopped.
