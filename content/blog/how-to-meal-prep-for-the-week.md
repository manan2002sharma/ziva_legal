## The food safety rules first

Meal prep only works if the food is still safe on Thursday. From USDA's Food Safety and Inspection Service and FoodSafety.gov:

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Cool it quickly | Refrigerate cooked food within 2 hours, or within 1 hour if it's above 90°F (32°C) |
| Fridge temperature | 40°F (4°C) or below |
| Fridge time | Eat cooked leftovers within 3 to 4 days |
| Freezer temperature | 0°F (−18°C) or below |
| Freezer time | 3 to 4 months for best quality; frozen food stays safe indefinitely |
| Reheating | To 165°F (74°C), checked with a food thermometer |

FoodSafety.gov's chart gives longer freezer times for some foods, such as 2 to 6 months for cooked meat and poultry. Freezer times are about quality, not safety.

## A simple prep routine

1. **Plan first.** Know which meals you're prepping before you shop. See [how to make a weekly meal plan](/blog/how-to-make-a-weekly-meal-plan).
2. **Cook proteins in bulk.** Two or three: for example a tray of chicken thighs, a pot of lentils and a block of baked tofu.
3. **Cook one or two starches.** Rice, quinoa, potatoes or pasta.
4. **Roast a tray of vegetables.** Plus a box of raw vegetables that don't need cooking.
5. **Make one sauce.** A dressing or sauce turns the same base into different meals.
6. **Cool, portion and label.** Spread food out to cool quickly, portion into containers and write the date on each.
7. **Freeze the back half of the week.** Anything you'll eat after day 4 goes straight into the freezer. Move it to the fridge the night before.

## Prep once or twice a week?

The 3-to-4-day limit decides this. If you don't want to freeze, prep twice:

| Prep day | Covers |
| --- | --- |
| Sunday | Monday to Wednesday |
| Wednesday evening | Thursday to Saturday |

Breakfasts like overnight oats and snacks like boiled eggs can be prepped in their own short batches.
