## What an AI meal planner should do

A meal planner earns its place when it decides the week for you. The useful ones take your calorie target, diet and the food you like, write a week of meals with recipes and macros, and turn them into a grocery list. The better ones let you change a meal without rebuilding everything.

One option is gone: Mealime shut down on October 21, 2026, so it isn't on this list.

## Eat This Much: the original automatic planner

Eat This Much builds meal plans from your diet goals, preferred foods, budget and schedule. Premium plans the whole week and emails it with a grocery list; the free tier plans a day at a time. You can also log what you eat for free.

**Best for:** people who want to hit calorie and macro numbers without thinking about it. **Watch out for:** no workout planning, and family members with different targets aren't individually optimized.

## Fitia: meal plans plus logging

Fitia creates meal plans around calorie and macro targets, builds a shopping list and lets you log food by photo, voice, text or barcode in Premium. The free tier is limited to four meals a day and part of the food database.

**Best for:** people who want planning and tracking in one app.

## Samsung Food: the best free option

Samsung Food's free version plans your week and turns it into a shareable shopping list, with grocery delivery from partner retailers. Food+ adds AI meal plans for your health goals and nutrition tracking for $6.99 a month.

**Best for:** families and recipe lovers on a budget.

## MyFitnessPal Premium+: planning where your logs are

MyFitnessPal's meal planner, recipes and grocery-app syncing are only in Premium+, its top tier. If you already track food there, adding the planner keeps everything in one place.

## Siloh: meals cycled around your training

Siloh, an iPhone app launched in July 2026, builds a 14-day meal plan and a 4-week training program together, with more carbs on training days than on rest days. Pro adds an AI coach you can chat with, including photos of a menu or a meal. Food logging, recipes and a grocery list you can send to Kroger or Instacart are in the free tier.

## ZivaAI: meals, workouts and groceries in one plan

ZivaAI writes a full week from a few questions: breakfast, lunch, dinner and snacks with recipes and macros for each meal, across 12 cuisines and diets including vegetarian, vegan, halal and kosher. It plans workouts of 20 to 60 minutes for your equipment in the same plan, and builds one grocery list sorted by aisle.

What sets it apart is the chat. Ask Ziva to swap Sunday's dinner or make lunches quicker, see the change before it happens, and the grocery list updates with it. You can also log what you actually ate: tick a planned meal, or photograph or describe anything else for a calorie and macro estimate.

## How to choose

- **Cheapest good weekly planner:** Samsung Food (free) or Eat This Much ($59.99 a year).
- **Plans plus food logging:** Fitia, or MyFitnessPal Premium+ if you already log there.
- **Meals and training in one plan, with a free tier:** Siloh.
- **One plan for meals and training that you can change by chatting:** ZivaAI.
