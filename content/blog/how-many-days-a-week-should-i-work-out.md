## What the guidelines say

The *Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans* (2nd edition) set two targets for adults:

- **Aerobic activity:** 150 to 300 minutes a week of moderate activity, or 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous activity, or a mix. Preferably spread across the week.
- **Muscle strengthening:** on 2 or more days a week, working all major muscle groups.

They also say some activity is better than none, and that going beyond 300 minutes a week brings additional benefits. The 2nd edition is still current; HHS is preparing a 2027 supplement on step counts and muscle-strengthening that adds to it rather than replacing it.

For strength training, the American College of Sports Medicine's 2026 update says to train all major muscle groups at least twice a week.

## Example weeks

Each of these meets both targets. Moderate cardio means things like brisk walking or easy cycling; vigorous means running, hard cycling or similar.

| Workouts a week | Strength | Cardio | Aerobic total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | 3 full-body sessions | 25 minutes vigorous after each | 75 min vigorous |
| 4 | 2 full-body sessions | 2 sessions of 75 minutes moderate | 150 min moderate |
| 5 | 2 full-body sessions | 3 sessions of 50 minutes moderate | 150 min moderate |
| 6 | 4 sessions: upper, lower, upper, lower | 2 sessions of 40 minutes vigorous | 80 min vigorous |

## How many days should you lift?

ACSM's 2009 position stand on resistance training gave frequencies by experience:

| Level | Lifting days per week |
| --- | --- |
| Beginner | 2 to 3 |
| Intermediate | 3 to 4 |
| Advanced | 4 to 5 |

More days usually means each session trains fewer muscles. What matters for muscle growth is weekly sets per muscle, covered in [how many sets and reps to build muscle](/blog/how-many-sets-and-reps-to-build-muscle).

## Rest days

Rest days don't have to be inactive. Walking counts toward your weekly minutes. Avoid training the same muscle group hard on back-to-back days; splitting upper and lower body, or alternating strength and cardio, handles that. See [full body vs split workouts](/blog/full-body-vs-split-workout).
