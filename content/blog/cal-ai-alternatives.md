## What Cal AI does

Cal AI is a calorie tracker built around the camera. You answer a few questions to get calorie and macro targets, then log a meal by taking a photo, scanning a barcode or describing it. Its website says it uses the phone's depth sensor to estimate how much food is on the plate. It runs on iPhone, Apple Watch and Android, has a 3-day free trial, and its App Store listing shows a 4.8 rating from about 375,000 ratings.

Food scanning results need a subscription. The App Store lists several "Unlimited" plans, priced from $2.99 to $29.99.

## MacroFactor: targets that adjust to you

MacroFactor logs food by AI photo, a written description, voice, barcode or nutrition label, from a verified database of more than 1.36 million foods. Its main difference is the coaching: it estimates how many calories you actually burn from your logs and weight, and adjusts your targets at weekly check-ins. There's a free trial but no free tier. It costs $11.99 a month, $47.99 for six months or $71.99 a year.

**Best for:** people who log consistently and want the targets to keep up with them.

## Lose It!: a free tier with photo logging in Premium

Lose It! gives you a calorie budget and free food and exercise tracking, searching a database of more than 56 million foods and recipes. Premium adds photo meal logging, AI voice logging, the barcode scanner, macro and nutrient tracking, fasting and meal targets. Its App Store listing shows several Premium prices, from $9.99 to $79.99, plus a lifetime option.

**Best for:** people who want to start free and add photo logging later.

## SnapCalorie: portion measurement from the camera

SnapCalorie logs from a photo or a voice note. It says it measures portions with the iPhone's camera and depth sensors, then pulls nutrition data from the USDA database, covering calories, macros and more than 100 nutrients. Premium is $19.99 a month or $149 a year.

**Best for:** people who mainly eat home-cooked meals without labels and want the portion size estimated.

## MyFitnessPal: the biggest food diary

MyFitnessPal's food and exercise logging is free. Premium adds barcode scanning, meal scan and voice logging for $19.99 a month or $79.99 a year, and Premium+ adds meal planning for $24.99 a month or $99.99 a year. It also has an AI Coach for questions about your logs.

**Best for:** people who want the largest food database and free logging.

## Cronometer: accuracy and micronutrients

Cronometer tracks calories, macros and more than 95 vitamins and minerals from a curated database of more than 1.1 million verified foods. Barcode scanning is free; photo and voice logging come with Gold, at $10.99 a month or $59.99 a year.

**Best for:** people who care about vitamins and minerals, not just calories.

## ZivaAI: photo logging plus a plan

ZivaAI plans your week of meals, with calories and macros for each, plus workouts and a grocery list. To log, tick a planned meal as eaten, or photograph or describe anything else and Ziva estimates its calories, protein, carbs and fat. It also logs your sets and keeps daily progress photos.

## Which should you choose?

- **Targets that adjust each week:** MacroFactor.
- **Start free:** Lose It! or Cronometer.
- **Portion sizes estimated from the photo:** SnapCalorie.
- **The biggest database and free logging:** MyFitnessPal.
- **A meal and workout plan, with photo logging beside it:** ZivaAI.
